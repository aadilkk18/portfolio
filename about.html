About

I build things that have to work.

I'm Aadil Kamal Khan, a Computer Science graduate focused on AI, software engineering and intelligent systems. I enjoy asking better questions before writing code, breaking complexity into clarity, and creating technology that is purposeful, practical and built for real-world impact, while holding it to a standard as close to perfection as possible.

The rule I keep coming back to

Look across my projects and you'll find the same move. I don't ask a model to behave. I build the system so it can't misbehave.

  • Kairos: the language model explains, but a quantitative engine does every calculation.
  • LEO: the model never supplies Khowar. Every word comes from a database I taught it.
  • HireLens: the model rates six categories, and plain code computes the final score.
  • Spinzar Gems: records can't be hard-deleted, and a stale edit is rejected instead of overwriting someone's work.

That's what reliable AI means to me: guarantees in code, not hopes in a prompt.

How I work

  • I'm picky about my own code. Not other people's, mine. If something already works but I know how it should look, I'll reopen it. So for me a system needs to be a 9, a 10, a 9 and a half but not a 6, not even an 8.
  • If something annoys me twice, I automate it.
  • I'm a quick learner, always ready to adapt to something new. Give me the problem, and I'll learn what it needs along the way.

Why SpecOne?

In 2004, a week after winning the Champions League with Porto, José Mourinho walked into his first press conference as Chelsea's new manager and said, "I think I'm a special one." Confidence only counts when the work backs it up. I'm a football fan, and the line stuck: I'm still earning mine.

SpecOne is the name I put on my work. For now it's the tag on this site. The plan is to turn it into a brand, then into a company: a technology company that builds AI products and software people rely on, and grows into something big.
