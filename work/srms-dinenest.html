SRMS / DineNest

A multi-tenant restaurant management system and our final year project. One installation that runs many restaurants. Each has its own menu, staff, orders and money, and none of them can see the others.

The key decision
One installation, many restaurants, no shared data.

The brief

A restaurant runs on a dozen things at once: the menu, the tables, the kitchen, the suppliers, the cash. DineNest puts all of it in one system. It is also built so a single installation can serve many restaurants without ever mixing their data.

It was our final year project, built by the three of us.

How multi-tenancy works

  1. One account, many restaurants.A user can belong to several restaurants.
  2. One is active at a time.The active restaurant is kept in the user’s session.
  3. Every protected page checks it.A check runs on every protected route, and every query is filtered by restaurant.
Example: a user with two restaurants

Switch from Restaurant A to Restaurant B and the menu, orders, staff and finances all change with it. Neither restaurant ever sees the other’s records.

A day in the restaurant

  • Taking an order: dine-in, takeaway or delivery, with a table and a waiter assigned.
  • Paying: cash, card or digital, with the change calculated.
  • Following it: a stepper on the order page shows Pending, Preparing, Ready and Completed. Cancelled orders get their own marker instead.
  • Printing: receipts go to a thermal printer, with a customer copy and a kitchen copy.
  • Stock: each item has a low-stock threshold, and the dashboard shows an alert listing what is running out.

Behind the counter

Suppliers, purchases and the payments against them. Expenses by category. Reports for finance, purchases, orders, customers and items, each downloadable as PDF or CSV and respecting the filters on screen. A dashboard shows revenue, orders and customers, the best seller of the week, and 7-day and 6-month charts.

Who can do what

There are two default roles, Super Admin and Admin, with fine-grained permissions such as “create orders” or “delete purchases”. They are hidden in the interface and also enforced on the server. Every significant action goes into an activity log, so there is a trail of who did what and when.

Bugs we’re glad we hit

A price showing as 28.979999999999997

A JavaScript rounding artifact in the payment window. We fixed it by rounding the value for display.

A Super Admin with a fraction of the permissions

Two seeder lists were silently cut short, so the top role could do far less than intended, and nothing showed an error.

Exports that crashed

The activity-logging middleware called a method that some response types do not have. We replaced it with one that every response supports.
